MEDTRUST AI
RELIABILITY-AWARE CLINICAL DECISION ROUTING

============================================================

1. RESEARCH OVERVIEW

MedTrust AI investigates whether clinical AI reliability should be assessed using predictive confidence alone or by combining predictive confidence with explanation reliability.

The proposed framework combines a calibrated prediction confidence score with an Explanation Reliability Score (ERS) and uses the combined signal to route predictions to ACCEPT, REVIEW, or DEFER.

============================================================

2. RESEARCH PROBLEM

A model can produce a high-confidence prediction while its explanation may be unstable or unreliable. Therefore, confidence alone may not completely describe whether a prediction should be routinely accepted.

MedTrust AI studies explanation reliability as a second reliability signal.

============================================================

10. RESEARCH INTERPRETATION


The experiment demonstrates a prototype reliability-aware routing mechanism in which predictive confidence is not treated as the only reliability signal.

The additional explanation-reliability signal can identify cases where predictive confidence and explanation reliability do not agree.

Such cases can be routed for additional human review instead of being automatically accepted.

============================================================

11. LIMITATIONS


- The current experiment uses the MIMIC-IV DEMO dataset.

- The number of mortality-positive cases is very small.

- Therefore performance metrics can be unstable.

- ERS components are a research prototype and require further validation.

- Routing thresholds and the 50/50 joint-reliability weight are prototype choices for this demonstration.

- The results should not be interpreted as evidence for clinical deployment.

============================================================

============================================================

13. CONCLUSION


MedTrust AI presents a reliability-aware clinical decision routing framework that combines calibrated predictive confidence with explanation reliability. The prototype demonstrates how predictions can be categorized into ACCEPT, REVIEW and DEFER rather than relying exclusively on model confidence.

The current results are intended as a methodological demonstration on the available MIMIC-IV demo dataset. Full-dataset and external validation are required before drawing clinical conclusions.
